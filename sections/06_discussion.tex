\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the benchmark tests}
Problem~S compares the anisotropic, active, nearly incompressible solid
with nine independent numerical solutions. Problems~F and~C add the fluid
while each keeps a solid-only reference problem with the same waveform.
Problem~F exposes the coupling to the added-mass effect under passive
loading and produces the filling vortex; Problem~C drives the fluid out by
fibre-driven contraction, with wall thickening and twist that challenge the
mesh motion. The coupled problems are verified by their own convergence and
conservation evidence; the coupled-minus-solid-only difference is a
comparison of two loading models and is reported as such.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
These problems are intended for numerical verification and
reproducibility, not for the reconstruction of a cardiac cycle. The
idealised geometry, the separate loading phases and the permanently open
ports provide controlled conditions for comparing numerical solutions;
flow reversal at the open port is permitted unless excluded by the stated
boundary condition. The geometry is that of AR25, defined numerically
rather than from imaging; its unloaded cavity volume of \SI{168}{mL} is
not a loaded clinical volume and is not given an anatomical
interpretation. The passive pressure waveform of AR25 reaches systolic
levels, so Problem~F is a stress test with that waveform, not physiological
filling. Blood is Newtonian and no turbulence model is used; activation is
prescribed rather than electrophysiology-driven; the viscous term of the
solid is first order in time. A closed-cycle benchmark with a
lumped-parameter circulation, and a cross-code comparison, are natural
next steps.
