\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

% Para 1 -- why LV FSI
Computational models of the heart are moving from research tools towards
clinical and regulatory use \citep{Quarteroni2017,Chabiniok2016}. Among
them, fluid--structure interaction (FSI) models of the left ventricle (LV)
resolve the two-way coupling between the contracting myocardium and the
intraventricular blood, and are used to study diastolic vortex formation,
intraventricular pressure differences, washout and the haemodynamic
consequences of altered wall mechanics \citep{Mittal2016}.

% Para 2 -- reproducibility problem (bounded claim)
The credibility of such models rests on verification: showing that the
discrete solution converges to the solution of the stated mathematical
model \citep{Roache1998,Oberkampf2010}. In LV FSI this is difficult to
assess from the literature, because the information needed to reproduce a
study is often incomplete. \Cref{tab:survey} records, for a defined set of
two-way myocardial LV FSI studies, whether the ventricular dimensions, a
mesh study, a time-step study, the discretisation schemes, the
stabilisation and the coupling tolerances are reported.

% Para 3 -- benchmarks that exist
Benchmarks have played this role elsewhere. For general FSI, the
\citet{TurekHron2006} channel-flow problem and the experimental benchmark
of \citet{Hessenthaler2017} are widely used, but neither involves an
active, anisotropic, nearly incompressible solid enclosing a cavity. For
cardiac solid mechanics, \citet{Land2015} proposed passive inflation and
active contraction of an idealised LV, and \citet{Arostica2025} extended
this to elastodynamics with a visco-active Holzapfel--Ogden material,
analytical fibres and pericardial supports, with solutions from nine
groups. Two recent experimental frameworks measure wall motion and flow
together in compliant ventricular phantoms --- an immersed-particle FSI
method validated against a deformable-chamber benchmark
\citep{Monteleone2026} and simultaneous Lagrangian measurement of wall and
flow in a patient-averaged LV \citep{Hadar2026} --- and provide validation
data for passive, isotropic chambers. We are not aware of a fully
specified computational benchmark for two-way FSI of an active, anisotropic
ventricle with a released reference configuration; the claim is bounded by
the survey of \cref{tab:survey}.

% Para 4 -- difficulties specific to cardiac FSI
Cardiac FSI is numerically demanding. The solid is nearly incompressible,
anisotropic and active, and collocated discretisations of it need
stabilisation whose magnitude can stiffen the response. Blood and
myocardium have similar densities, so partitioned schemes are exposed to
the added-mass effect \citep{Causin2005}, although the density ratio alone
does not quantify it. When the cavity is closed by purely kinematic
boundary conditions, the enclosed incompressible fluid creates a
compatibility problem for standard partitioned iterations
\citep{Kuttler2006}. The settings chosen to deal with these issues ---
stabilisation factors, interface conditions, residual definitions,
relaxation and tolerances --- change the answer, and they must be reported
for a solution to be reproducible.

% Para 5 -- contribution
Building on our solids4foam implementation of the \citet{Arostica2025}
benchmark \citep{s4fPR571}, this paper defines a reproducible extension to
FSI in an idealised LV. Rather than a cardiac cycle, it poses two
valve-free problems --- pressure-driven passive deformation and
activation-driven contraction --- each the counterpart of a solid-only AR25
case. The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a fully specified coupled problem --- geometry and port dimensions,
  fibres, solid and fluid models with all equations and parameters written
  out, interface and boundary conditions, initial conditions and forcing
  tables --- that requires no valve model (\cref{sec:problem});
\item an executable reference configuration, with the solid part inherited
  from the verified implementation and the fluid discretisation, mesh
  motion, interface transfer, coupling sequence, residual definitions,
  acceleration and tolerances stated explicitly and exported as a settings
  manifest (\cref{sec:methods});
\item a verification methodology that separates solid cross-code
  comparison, implementation checks and FSI solution verification, with
  convergence classification and grid-convergence indices per quantity of
  interest, conservation diagnostics, and a robustness study of the
  numerical settings (\cref{sec:verification});
\item \RES{the reference data and their estimated uncertainty}
  (\cref{sec:results});
\item open cases and data at a fixed commit, and a minimum reporting
  checklist for ventricular FSI (\cref{app:checklist}).
\end{enumerate}
A single-code reference data set is labelled as a candidate reference until
independent solutions confirm it; a cross-code comparison, as in AR25, is
a natural follow-up and is not a prerequisite for the problem definition.
