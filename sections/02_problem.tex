\section{Benchmark problem definition}\label{sec:problem}

All quantities are in SI units. The solid sub-problem is that of Benchmark~1
of \citet{Arostica2025} (hereafter AR25), restated here in full so that the
manuscript is self-contained; the implementation is the solids4foam case of
PR~\#571 \citep{s4fPR571}. Where the present text and AR25 differ in
notation, AR25's symbols are given in brackets.

% ===========================================================================
\subsection{Geometry}\label{sec:geometry}

\paragraph{Myocardium $\Os$} The truncated ellipsoid of AR25 (their eqs.~9--11),
with long axis along $x$ and apex at $x<0$:
\begin{equation}
\vect{x}(\mu,\theta) = \bigl(r_{\mathrm{long}}\cos\mu,\;
 r_{\mathrm{short}}\sin\mu\cos\theta,\;
 r_{\mathrm{short}}\sin\mu\sin\theta\bigr),\qquad \theta\in[-\pi,\pi],
\end{equation}
with the dimensions of \cref{tab:geometry}. The endocardial and epicardial
rims lie in two different planes ($x=\SI{26.47}{mm}$ and $x=\SI{24.25}{mm}$);
the solid base $\Gbase$ is the annular surface joining them by a straight
line in each meridian plane, so it is a shallow cone, not a plane.
For the endocardial ellipsoid, with $a=r_{\mathrm{long}}$, $b=r_{\mathrm{short}}$
and $c = 5a/17$ the rim coordinate, the enclosed volume between the apex and
the rim plane is
$V_0 = \pi b^2\bigl[c - c^3/(3a^2) + 2a/3\bigr] = \SI{168.29}{mL}$.

\paragraph{Fluid domain $\Of$} The cavity enclosed by $\Gendo$ and closed at
the endocardial rim by a cap $\Gcap$. The cap contains an inflow (mitral)
port $\Gin$ and an outflow (aortic) port $\Gout$, both circular; the rest of
the cap, $\Gcap\setminus(\Gin\cup\Gout)$, is an impermeable wall.

\AUTHOR{Cap kinematics. The solid base rests on springs
($\alpha_{\mathrm{top}}=\SI{1e5}{Pa/m}$), so the endocardial rim moves
axially and non-uniformly during loading, while the ports are most easily
kept fixed in space. A planar cap with fixed ports and a rim that follows
the endocardium is not kinematically consistent. Three options:
(i) the cap is a deformable artificial fluid wall whose nodes move with a
prescribed interpolation between the moving rim and the fixed port edges
(wall velocity equal to the cap velocity; no structural stiffness; the
fluid reaction on the cap is not transmitted to the solid);
(ii) ports move rigidly with the mean rim displacement, and the cap is
planar in the moving frame;
(iii) the rim is constrained (e.g.\ $u_x=0$ on $\Gbase$), in which case the
solid problem is no longer AR25's and a solid-only control with the same
constraint must be added, with the original AR25 case kept as a separate
verification. First inspect the basal displacement in Problem~S, then
decide. State the chosen map $\vect x_{\mathrm{cap}}(\vect X,t)$ and
$\ww$ on $\Gcap$ explicitly.}

\AUTHOR{Ports. Radii, centres and whether short tubes are extruded from
the cap. With the proposed values the radial clearance to the rim is
\SI{1.4}{mm} (inflow) and \SI{1.9}{mm} (outflow) and the edge-to-edge gap is
\SI{4.5}{mm}; these narrow regions need mesh-quality attention and do not
guarantee clearance after deformation. Tube length is a proposal (a few
diameters) until boundary independence is shown.}

\subsection{Fibre architecture}\label{sec:fibres}
Fibres $\f$ and sheets $\s$ follow AR25 Section~3.2: a transmural
coordinate $\bar t$ from $\Delta\bar t=0$ ($\bar t=0$ on $\Gendo$, $1$ on
$\Gepi$, zero flux on $\Gbase$), the ellipsoidal basis $(\vect e_\mu,\vect e_\theta)$
with radii interpolated linearly in $\bar t$, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\f &= \sin\varphi\,\vect e_\mu + \cos\varphi\,\vect e_\theta,\qquad
\vect n_0 = \frac{\vect e_\mu\times\vect e_\theta}{\|\vect e_\mu\times\vect e_\theta\|},\qquad
\s = \frac{\f\times\vect n_0}{\|\f\times\vect n_0\|},\\
\varphi(\bar t) &= \varphi_{\mathrm{endo}} + (\varphi_{\mathrm{epi}}-\varphi_{\mathrm{endo}})\,\bar t,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with helix angles $\varphi_{\mathrm{endo}}=-60^\circ$,
$\varphi_{\mathrm{epi}}=+60^\circ$ (AR25: $\alpha_{\mathrm{endo}},\alpha_{\mathrm{epi}}$;
renamed here to avoid a clash with the epicardial spring stiffness
$\alpha_{\mathrm{epi}}$). Orthonormality of $(\f,\s,\vect n_0)$ and the
treatment at the apex, where $\vect e_\theta$ is undefined, are checked
numerically (\cref{sec:ver:impl}).

\subsection{Solid model}\label{sec:solid}
With $\FF=\II+\Grad\uu$, $J=\det\FF$, $\CC=\FF^{\mathsf T}\FF$,
$\EE=\tfrac12(\CC-\II)$ and $\PP=\FF\SSS$, the displacement satisfies
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{s}}\,\ddot{\uu} - \Div\PP = \vect 0 \quad\text{in } \Os.
\end{equation}
The second Piola--Kirchhoff stress is (AR25 eq.~2)
\begin{equation}
\SSS = \frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\EE} + \eta\,\dot{\EE} + \tau(t)\,\f\otimes\f,
\label{eq:S}
\end{equation}
with the invariants
$\bar I_1 = J^{-2/3}\operatorname{tr}\CC$,
$I_{4f} = \f\cdot\CC\f$, $I_{4s} = \s\cdot\CC\s$, $I_{8fs} = \f\cdot\CC\s$
(only the isotropic invariant is isochoric) and the energy (AR25 eq.~3)
\begin{align}
\Psi &= \frac{a}{2b}\bigl[e^{b(\bar I_1-3)}-1\bigr]
 + \sum_{i\in\{f,s\}}\frac{a_i}{2b_i}\,\chi(I_{4i})\bigl[e^{b_i(I_{4i}-1)^2}-1\bigr]
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad + \frac{a_{fs}}{2b_{fs}}\bigl[e^{b_{fs}I_{8fs}^2}-1\bigr]
 + \frac{\kappa}{4}\bigl[J^2-1-2\ln J\bigr],
\label{eq:Psi}\\
\chi(x) &= \frac{1}{1+e^{-k(x-1)}},\qquad k = 100.
\end{align}
The smooth switch $\chi$ is the logistic approximation proposed by AR25 to
the Heaviside switch of their printed energy; the two are not identical
functions, and the benchmark specifies the logistic form, including the
derivative $\chi'$ in $\partial\Psi/\partial\EE$. The constant $-1$ in the
first bracket fixes the energy at $\CC=\II$ and does not change the stress.
The volumetric term gives $U'(J) = \tfrac{\kappa}{2}(J-J^{-1})$; the
material is nearly, not exactly, incompressible, and $J-1$ is reported as a
QoI.

\paragraph{Active stress} With $\Splus(z) = \tfrac12[1+\tanh(z/\gamma)]$,
$\Sminus(z) = \tfrac12[1-\tanh(z/\gamma)]$ and $\pos{x}=\max(x,0)$
(AR25 eqs.~5--6; the activation function is written $\aact$ here to avoid
confusion with the material coefficient $a$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f(t) &= \Splus(t-t_{\mathrm{sys}})\,\Sminus(t-t_{\mathrm{dias}}),\qquad
\aact(t) = \alpha_{\max} f(t) + \alpha_{\min}\bigl[1-f(t)\bigr],\\
\dot\tau &= -|\aact(t)|\,\tau + \sigma_0\,\pos{\aact(t)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
with $\tau(0)=0$, $\sigma_0=\SI{1.5e5}{Pa}$, $\gamma=\SI{0.005}{s}$,
$\alpha_{\min}=-30$, $\alpha_{\max}=5$, $t_{\mathrm{sys}}=\SI{0.16}{s}$,
$t_{\mathrm{dias}}=\SI{0.484}{s}$. AR25 tabulate $\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}$
without units; dimensionally they are rates, and the benchmark takes them in
\si{s^{-1}} with $t$ in seconds.

\paragraph{Passive pressure waveform} (AR25 eqs.~7--8, their Table~4;
monoventricular values, not the biventricular Table~9):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{pre}}(t) &= \Splus(t-t_{\mathrm{sys\text{-}pre}})\,\Sminus(t-t_{\mathrm{dias\text{-}pre}}),\qquad
g_{\mathrm{pre}}(t) = \Sminus(t-t_{\mathrm{dias\text{-}pre}}),\\
b_{\mathrm{act}}(t) &= \alpha_{\max} f_{\mathrm{pre}} + \alpha_{\min}[1-f_{\mathrm{pre}}]
 + \alpha_{\mathrm{pre}}\,g_{\mathrm{pre}} + \alpha_{\mathrm{mid}},\\
\dot p &= -|b_{\mathrm{act}}(t)|\,p + \sigma_{\mathrm{mid}}\pos{b_{\mathrm{act}}(t)}
 + \sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}\pos{g_{\mathrm{pre}}(t)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:p}
\end{equation}
with $p(0)=0$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{pre}}=5$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}=1$ (\si{s^{-1}}),
$\sigma_{\mathrm{pre}}=\SI{7000}{Pa}$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{mid}}=\SI{16000}{Pa}$,
$t_{\mathrm{sys\text{-}pre}}=\SI{0.17}{s}$, $t_{\mathrm{dias\text{-}pre}}=\SI{0.484}{s}$.

\paragraph{Tabulated forcing} Both ODEs are integrated once by the
\texttt{makeArosticaTimeSeries} utility of PR~\#571 (exponential update with
coefficients frozen at the sub-step midpoint, internal step \SI{1e-5}{s},
output every \SI{1e-4}{s}, linear interpolation between sub-steps) and the
tables are released with the case. The utility gives peaks
$\tau_{\max}=\SI{118106}{Pa}$ and $p_{\max}=\SI{16074}{Pa}$, against
\SI{118817}{Pa} and \SI{16118}{Pa} quoted in AR25's figure captions.

\subsection{Fluid model}\label{sec:fluid}
Blood is an incompressible Newtonian fluid. In ALE form on the moving domain
$\Of$ with mesh velocity $\ww$:
\begin{align}
\rho_{\mathrm{f}}\Bigl(\left.\frac{\partial\vv}{\partial t}\right|_{\chi}
 + \bigl((\vv-\ww)\cdot\nabla\bigr)\vv\Bigr)
 - \dive\sig_{\mathrm{f}} &= \vect 0, &
\dive\vv &= 0 \quad\text{in } \Of,\\
\sig_{\mathrm{f}} &= -p\,\II + 2\mu_{\mathrm{f}}\tens D,\qquad
\tens D = \operatorname{sym}(\nabla\vv),
\end{align}
with $\rho_{\mathrm f}=\SI{1050}{kg/m^3}$ and
$\mu_{\mathrm f}=\SI{3.5e-3}{Pa.s}$ ($\nu_{\mathrm f} = \SI{3.33e-6}{m^2/s}$).
Gravity is neglected. No turbulence model is used; the Reynolds number
$Re=\rho_{\mathrm f}U D/\mu_{\mathrm f}$, with $U$ the peak port mean velocity
and $D$ the port diameter, is reported as a characterisation of each
problem, not as proof of laminar flow (the tests are not periodic, so no
Womersley number is defined).

\subsection{Interface and boundary conditions}\label{sec:bcs}

\paragraph{Fluid--solid interface $\Gfsi=\Gendo$} Let $\nn_{\mathrm f}$ and
$\nn_{\mathrm s}$ be the outward unit normals of the fluid and solid domains
on the current interface, $\nn_{\mathrm s}=-\nn_{\mathrm f}$, and $\NN_{\mathrm s}$
the outward solid normal in the reference configuration, related by Nanson's
formula $\nn_{\mathrm s}\,\mathrm da = J\FF^{-\mathsf T}\NN_{\mathrm s}\,\mathrm dA$.
Kinematic and dynamic continuity read
\begin{equation}
\vv = \dot{\uu},\qquad
\sig_{\mathrm f}\nn_{\mathrm f} + \sig_{\mathrm s}\nn_{\mathrm s} = \vect 0
\quad\text{on }\Gfsi,\qquad \sig_{\mathrm s} = J^{-1}\PP\FF^{\mathsf T},
\label{eq:interface}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the solid receives the traction $\sig_{\mathrm f}\nn_{\mathrm f}$ per
unit current area, and a positive fluid pressure $p$ compresses the wall.
This replaces the prescribed endocardial pressure of AR25.

\paragraph{Solid} On $\Gepi$ and $\Gbase$ the Robin (spring--dashpot)
conditions of AR25 eq.~(1): on $\Gepi$ the normal component only, with
$\alpha_{\mathrm{epi}}=\SI{1e8}{Pa/m}$, $\beta_{\mathrm{epi}}=\SI{5e3}{Pa.s/m}$,
and on $\Gbase$ all components with $\alpha_{\mathrm{top}}=\SI{1e5}{Pa/m}$,
$\beta_{\mathrm{top}}=\SI{5e3}{Pa.s/m}$.

\paragraph{Fluid} On the cap wall, $\vv=\ww$ (no slip relative to the moving
wall; $\ww=\vect0$ where the cap is fixed). The port conditions depend on
the problem (\cref{sec:steps}). A closed port is a wall. An open port
carries either a prescribed normal traction
$\sig_{\mathrm f}\nn_{\mathrm f} = -p_{\mathrm{port}}(t)\,\nn_{\mathrm f}$
with zero tangential velocity gradient (pressure-driven), or a prescribed
volumetric flow rate $Q(t)$, defined positive \emph{into} the cavity, with a
plug profile (flow-driven). An open pressure port is bidirectional: nothing
prevents inflow through the nominal outlet or outflow through the nominal
inlet, in particular during relaxation after contraction or while the
prescribed pressure falls. The benchmark permits this and reports both net
and gross port volumes (\cref{sec:qoi}).
\AUTHOR{Treatment of reverse flow at open pressure boundaries (none, or an
inflow stabilisation term) is part of the problem definition and must be
fixed and stated.}

\paragraph{Initial conditions} $\uu=\dot\uu=\vect0$, $\vv=\vect0$, $p=0$,
$\tau(0)=p_{\mathrm{port}}(0)=0$, mesh in the reference configuration. Since
all forcing starts from zero, the initial state is an equilibrium and no
ramp or pre-stress is applied. The time origin is that of AR25.

\subsection{Benchmark problems}\label{sec:steps}
The cardiac cycle is not simulated. Filling and contraction are posed as
two separate problems on $t\in[0,1]$\,s, each starting from the reference
configuration at rest, and each the counterpart of an AR25 Step~0 case
(\cref{tab:problems}). Only one port is open in each problem, so no valve
model is needed.

\begin{description}
\item[{Problem S --- solid only.}]
  AR25 Benchmark~1, Steps~0A, 0B and~1, with the prescribed endocardial
  pressure of AR25 and no fluid, solved with the solid solver, meshes and
  settings used in Problems F and C. Compared with the nine AR25 numerical
  solutions (\cref{sec:ver:solid}).
\item[{Problem F --- pressure-driven passive deformation.}] $\tau\equiv0$.
  $\Gin$ open, $\Gout$ a wall. Core variant F-p: $p_{\mathrm{port}}(t)=p(t)$ of
  \cref{eq:p}, the same waveform AR25 Step~0B prescribes over the whole
  endocardium. Extension F-Q: $Q_{\mathrm{in}}(t)$ prescribed, which imposes
  the cavity volume kinematically. \AUTHOR{F-Q waveform: profile, amplitude,
  duration, sign and compatibility with the initial rest state must be
  frozen and released; e.g.\ $Q_{\mathrm E}\sin^2(\pi t/t_{\mathrm f})$ on
  $[0,t_{\mathrm f}]$ and zero after.}
\item[{Problem C --- activation-driven contraction.}] $\tau(t)$ of
  \cref{eq:tau}. $\Gin$ a wall, $\Gout$ open. Core variant C-p:
  $p_{\mathrm{port}}=0$ (traction-free outlet), the counterpart of AR25
  Step~0A (active, $p=0$ on the endocardium). \AUTHOR{Optional C-R: resistive
  or three-element Windkessel outlet. Keep as supplementary/future work
  unless its equations, parameters and initialisation are fully specified
  and run.}
\end{description}

\paragraph{Meaning of the solid-only comparison}
In Problem~S the pressure acts uniformly over the whole endocardium. In the
coupled problems the pressure (or zero traction) is imposed at a port, and
the endocardial traction --- a spatially varying pressure plus viscous shear
--- follows from the fluid solution; the cap and port geometry also enter.
The difference between the coupled and the solid-only displacement is
therefore a comparison of two loading models under the chosen boundary
configuration. It is not an FSI verification error, and it does not by
itself separate the contributions of fluid inertia, viscosity or coupling
error. Three distinct activities are kept apart throughout:
(i)~solid cross-code comparison (Problem~S against the AR25 solutions);
(ii)~FSI solution verification (spatial, temporal and iterative convergence
and conservation of the coupled problems, \cref{sec:verification}); and
(iii)~physical-model comparison (coupled vs.\ solid-only response).
In F-p and C-p the cavity volume is an output, so partitioned coupling does
not face a pure-kinematic fluid boundary; F-Q, in which the enclosed volume is
prescribed and the cavity pressure is determined only by the solid, is the
harder test for partitioned iterations \citep{Kuttler2006}.
The passive pressure of \cref{eq:p} reaches \SI{16}{kPa}, a systolic rather
than a diastolic level; F-p is therefore a numerical stress test with the
AR25 waveform, not a physiological filling phase.

\subsection{Quantities of interest}\label{sec:qoi}
For each problem, with sampling every \SI{1}{ms} for all time series (the
\SI{10}{ms} AR25 sampling is used only for $\RED$):
\begin{enumerate}
\item displacements $\uu(\vect p_0,t)$, $\uu(\vect p_1,t)$ at the AR25
  material points $\vect p_0=(0.025,0.03,0)$\,m and $\vect p_1=(0,0.03,0)$\,m
  (interpolated from the cell-centred field with \TODO{interpolation}), and
  at the epicardial apex $\vect p_{\mathrm{apex}}=(-0.097,0,0)$\,m;
\item cavity volume $V(t)$ from the closed oriented surface
  $\Gendo\cup\Gcap$, $V=\tfrac13\oint\vect x\cdot\nn\,\mathrm da$ (if tubes
  are present, the tube volume is reported separately), and the port flow
  rate $Q(t)=-\int_{\mathrm{port}}(\vv-\ww)\cdot\nn_{\mathrm f}\,\mathrm da$,
  positive into the cavity;
\item net port volume $\int_0^t Q\,\mathrm ds$ and gross outward volume
  $\int_0^t\pos{-Q}\,\mathrm ds$ (these differ when flow reverses); the
  ALE mass residual
  $R_V(t) = V(t)-V(0)-\int_0^t Q\,\mathrm ds$, absolute and relative to $V_0$;
\item cavity-averaged pressure $\bar p(t)=|\Of|^{-1}\int_{\Of}p$, and the
  intraventricular pressure difference
  $\Delta p_{\mathrm{IVPD}}(t)=\bar p_{\mathrm{base}}-\bar p_{\mathrm{apex}}$
  between two spherical sampling regions of radius \SI{3}{mm} centred at
  \TODO{base point} and \SI{5}{mm} above the endocardial apex (region
  averages, not vertex values, so that the quantity is not method-sensitive);
  the pressure reference is $p_{\mathrm{port}}$ on the open port;
\item fluid kinetic energy $E_k(t)=\tfrac12\rho_{\mathrm f}\int_{\Of}|\vv|^2$
  and viscous dissipation $\Phi_{\mathrm f}(t)=\int_{\Of}2\mu_{\mathrm f}\tens D:\tens D$;
\item $\max_{\Os}|J-1|$ over time;
\item for Problem F, in the plane $z=0$: the circulation of the filling
  vortex, $\Gamma=\int_{A}\omega_z\,\mathrm dA$ over the region $A$ where
  $\lambda_2<\lambda_{2,\mathrm{thr}}$ \TODO{threshold in \si{s^{-2}}}, and the
  position of its core (minimum of $\lambda_2$); the 2-D circulation and the
  3-D $\lambda_2$ criterion are reported as distinct diagnostics;
\item velocity profiles along the long axis and along the line
  $x=0$, $z=0$ at $t\in\{0.2,0.3,0.4,0.5\}$\,s;
\item peaks of each time series together with the time at which they occur,
  and values at fixed times, reported separately;
\item coupling and cost statistics: FSI iterations per time step, solid
  Newton iterations per FSI iteration, and wall-clock time with hardware
  and core count.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Discrepancy measure} For comparison with AR25 the relative
discrepancy of their eq.~(21) is used unchanged: with $N$ participant
solutions $\uu^i$ and their mean $\bar\uu$,
\begin{equation}
\RED^i(\vect p) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{n=0}^{T-1}
\frac{\|\uu^i(t_n,\vect p)-\bar\uu(t_n,\vect p)\|_2}{\|\bar\uu(t_n,\vect p)\|_2},
\qquad t_n = n\cdot\SI{10}{ms},\quad T=101,
\end{equation}
with linear interpolation of histories to $t_n$.
\CODECHECK{The denominator vanishes at $t_0=0$; adopt the convention of the
cardiac-benchmark-toolkit for zero/near-zero denominators and state how
multiple submissions from one group are weighted in $\bar\uu$.}
For convergence studies a separately named relative error against the
finest solution is used instead (\cref{sec:ver:error}); $\RED$ is never
redefined.
