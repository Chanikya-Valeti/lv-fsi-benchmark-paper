\section{Benchmark problem definition}\label{sec:problem}

All quantities are in SI units. Wherever possible the definitions of
\citet{Arostica2025} (hereafter AR25) are reused verbatim, so that the solid
sub-problem is identical to their Benchmark~1 and each FSI problem below has a
solid-only counterpart for which nine reference solutions already exist.

% ===========================================================================
\subsection{Geometry}\label{sec:geometry}

\paragraph{Myocardium $\Os$} The truncated ellipsoid of AR25 (their eqs.~9--11),
with long axis along $x$ and apex at $x<0$:
\begin{equation}
\vect{x}(\mu,\theta) = \bigl(r_{\mathrm{long}}\cos\mu,\;
 r_{\mathrm{short}}\sin\mu\cos\theta,\;
 r_{\mathrm{short}}\sin\mu\sin\theta\bigr),\qquad \theta\in[-\pi,\pi],
\end{equation}
with the dimensions of \cref{tab:geometry}. The base $\Gbase$ joins the
endocardial and epicardial rims by a straight line in each meridian plane.

\paragraph{Fluid domain $\Of$} The LV cavity enclosed by $\Gendo$ and closed at
the endocardial rim by the planar cap $\Gcap$ ($x = x_{\mathrm{cap}}$). The cap
contains an inflow (mitral) port $\Gin$ and an outflow (aortic) port $\Gout$,
both circular and fixed in space; the rest of the cap,
$\Gcap\setminus(\Gin\cup\Gout)$, is a no-slip wall.

\subsection{Fibre architecture}
Fibres $\f$ and sheets $\s$ follow AR25 Section~3.2 exactly: a transmural
coordinate $\bar t$ from $\Delta\bar t=0$ ($\bar t=0$ on $\Gendo$, $1$ on
$\Gepi$, zero flux on $\Gbase$), an ellipsoidal basis with interpolated radii,
and a helix angle $\alpha(\bar t) = -60^\circ + 120^\circ\,\bar t$.

% ===========================================================================
\subsection{Solid model}\label{sec:solid}
In the reference configuration $\Os$, the displacement $\uu$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{s}}\,\ddot{\uu} - \Div\PP = \vect 0 \quad\text{in } \Os,
\qquad \PP = \FF\SSS,
\end{equation}
with the visco-active Holzapfel--Ogden second Piola--Kirchhoff stress of AR25
(their eqs.~2--6):
\begin{equation}
\SSS = \frac{\partial\Psi_{\mathrm{aniso}}}{\partial\EE}
     + \eta\,\dot{\EE} + \tau(t)\,\f\otimes\f,
\end{equation}
where $\Psi_{\mathrm{aniso}}$ includes the smooth fibre-compression switch
($k=100$) and the volumetric penalty $\tfrac{\kappa}{4}(J^2-1-2\ln J)$, and the
active stress $\tau(t)$ solves AR25 eq.~(5) with the parameters of their
Table~3 (unchanged timing, $t_{\mathrm{sys}}=\SI{0.16}{s}$,
$t_{\mathrm{dias}}=\SI{0.484}{s}$). All parameters are listed in
\cref{app:parameters}.

% ===========================================================================
\subsection{Fluid model}\label{sec:fluid}
Blood is an incompressible Newtonian fluid. In ALE form on the moving domain
$\Of$ with mesh velocity $\ww$:
\begin{align}
\rho_{\mathrm{f}}\Bigl(\left.\frac{\partial\vv}{\partial t}\right|_{\chi}
 + \bigl((\vv-\ww)\cdot\nabla\bigr)\vv\Bigr)
 - \dive\sig_{\mathrm{f}} &= \vect 0, &
\dive\vv &= 0 \quad\text{in } \Of,\\
\sig_{\mathrm{f}} &= -p\,\II + \mu_{\mathrm{f}}\bigl(\nabla\vv + \nabla\vv^{\mathsf T}\bigr),
\end{align}
with $\rho_{\mathrm f}=\SI{1050}{kg/m^3}$ and
$\mu_{\mathrm f}=\SI{3.5e-3}{Pa.s}$ ($\nu_{\mathrm f} = \SI{3.33e-6}{m^2/s}$).
Gravity is neglected and the flow is assumed laminar.

\subsection{Interface and boundary conditions}\label{sec:bcs}

\paragraph{Fluid--solid interface $\Gfsi=\Gendo$} Kinematic and dynamic
continuity,
\begin{equation}
\vv = \dot{\uu},\qquad
\sig_{\mathrm f}\,\nn\,\mathrm da = \PP\NN\,\mathrm dA
\quad\text{on }\Gfsi,
\end{equation}
which replace the prescribed endocardial pressure of AR25.

\paragraph{Solid} On $\Gepi$ and $\Gbase$ the Robin (spring--dashpot)
conditions of AR25 (normal-only on $\Gepi$ with
$\alpha_{\mathrm{epi}}=\SI{1e8}{Pa/m}$, $\beta_{\mathrm{epi}}=\SI{5e3}{Pa.s/m}$;
all directions on $\Gbase$ with $\alpha_{\mathrm{top}}=\SI{1e5}{Pa/m}$,
$\beta_{\mathrm{top}}=\SI{5e3}{Pa.s/m}$).

\paragraph{Fluid} No slip on the cap wall. The port conditions depend on the
problem (\cref{sec:steps}). A closed port is a no-slip wall; an open port
carries either a prescribed normal traction
$\sig_{\mathrm f}\nn = -p_{\mathrm{port}}(t)\,\nn$ (pressure-driven variant) or a
prescribed volumetric flow rate $Q(t)$ with a plug \TODO{or parabolic}
profile (flow-driven variant).

\subsection{Benchmark problems}\label{sec:steps}
The cardiac cycle is \emph{not} simulated. Instead, filling and contraction are
posed as two separate problems, each starting from the unloaded reference
configuration at rest, $t\in[0,1]$\,s, and each the FSI counterpart of an AR25
Step~0 case (\cref{tab:problems}). Valves are not needed: in each problem only
one port is open.

\begin{description}
\item[{Problem S --- solid only (verification of the solid solver).}]
  AR25 Benchmark~1, Steps~0A, 0B and~1, solved with the same solid solver,
  meshes and settings used in Problems F and C. Results are compared with
  the nine AR25 reference codes.
\item[{Problem F --- passive filling.}] $\tau\equiv0$. $\Gin$ open, $\Gout$
  closed. Pressure-driven variant F-p: $p_{\mathrm{in}}(t)=p(t)$ of AR25
  eq.~(7), Table~4. This is the FSI counterpart of AR25 Step~0B (passive,
  endocardial pressure $p(t)$). Flow-driven variant F-Q: $Q_{\mathrm{in}}(t)$
  prescribed \TODO{analytical waveform, e.g.\
  $Q_{\mathrm E}\sin^2(\pi t/t_{\mathrm f})$}, which imposes the cavity
  volume kinematically.
\item[{Problem C --- active contraction.}] $\tau(t)$ of AR25 Table~3.
  $\Gin$ closed, $\Gout$ open. Pressure-driven variant C-p: traction-free
  outlet, $p_{\mathrm{out}}=0$. This is the FSI counterpart of AR25 Step~0A
  (active, $p=0$). \TODO{Optional variant C-R: resistive or three-element
  Windkessel outlet to provide a physiological afterload.}
\end{description}

\paragraph{Why pressure-driven variants} In F-p and C-p the cavity volume is an
output, so partitioned schemes do not face the incompressibility dilemma
\citep{Kuttler2006}, and each solution can be compared directly with the AR25
solid-only solution. The difference between the FSI and solid-only
displacements isolates the effect of fluid inertia and viscosity, i.e.\ of the
coupling itself. The flow-driven variant F-Q is the harder test for
partitioned coupling, because the enclosed fluid volume is prescribed and the
cavity pressure acts as a Lagrange multiplier. \TODO{Note: the AR25

diastolic level (e.g.\ \SI{1.33}{kPa}) and lose the direct comparison.}

\subsection{Quantities of interest}\label{sec:qoi}
For each problem:
\begin{enumerate}
\item displacements $\uu(\vect p_0,t)$, $\uu(\vect p_1,t)$ at the AR25 points
  $\vect p_0=(0.025,0.03,0)$\,m and $\vect p_1=(0,0.03,0)$\,m, and of the
  epicardial apex;
\item cavity volume $V(t)$, from the fluid mesh and independently from
  $\Gendo$ (consistency check), and the port flow rate;
\item mean cavity pressure $\bar p(t)$, apex pressure $p_{\mathrm{apex}}(t)$,
  and the intraventricular pressure difference
  $\Delta p_{\mathrm{IVPD}}(t) = p_{\mathrm{base}}-p_{\mathrm{apex}}$;
\item fluid kinetic energy $E_k(t) = \tfrac12\rho_{\mathrm f}\int_{\Of}|\vv|^2$
  and viscous dissipation $\Phi(t)$;
\item vortex metrics in the mid-plane $z=0$ (Problem F): circulation and
  vortex-core position \TODO{criterion: $\lambda_2$ or $Q$, threshold};
\item velocity profiles along prescribed lines at given times \TODO{define lines};
\item coupling and cost statistics: FSI iterations per time step, solid
  non-linear iterations, wall-clock time and cores.
\end{enumerate}
Discrepancy between solutions is measured with the relative discrepancy
$\RED$ of AR25 (their eq.~21), sampled every \SI{10}{ms}.
