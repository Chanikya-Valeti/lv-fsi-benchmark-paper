\begin{abstract}
Fluid--structure interaction (FSI) models of the left ventricle (LV) are
used to study intraventricular haemodynamics, yet the information needed to
reproduce them --- ventricular dimensions, constitutive parameters, spatial
and temporal schemes, stabilisation, the coupling algorithm, its residual
definitions and tolerances, and evidence of mesh and time-step convergence
--- is often incomplete in published studies. Community benchmarks exist for
cardiac solid mechanics \citep{Land2015,Arostica2025}; we are not aware of a
fully specified, openly released benchmark for two-way myocardial FSI.
Building on our solids4foam implementation of the \citet{Arostica2025}
cardiac elastodynamics benchmark, we define a reproducible extension to FSI
in an idealised left ventricle: a truncated-ellipsoid myocardium with an
analytical fibre field, a visco-active Holzapfel--Ogden law and pericardial
Robin supports, whose cavity is filled with an incompressible Newtonian
fluid. Rather than a cardiac cycle, two valve-free problems are posed:
pressure-driven passive deformation through an inflow port, and
activation-driven contraction through an outflow port. Each has a solid-only
counterpart in \citet{Arostica2025} with nine published numerical solutions,
which verify the solid solver; the coupled problems are verified by spatial,
temporal and iterative convergence and by mass and energy diagnostics, and
the difference between coupled and solid-only responses characterises the
influence of the fluid domain and boundary configuration. The contribution
is a fully specified coupled problem and an executable reference
configuration --- fluid discretisation, mesh motion, interface transfer,
coupling acceleration and convergence criteria, all stated explicitly ---
with sensitivity studies that quantify the numerical uncertainty of the
reported quantities of interest.

\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Extends the Ar\'ostica et al.\ (2025) cardiac elastodynamics benchmark to FSI.
\item Two valve-free problems: pressure-driven filling, activation-driven contraction.
\item Solid verified against nine published codes; coupled problems by convergence.
\item Coupling sequence, residuals, tolerances and stabilisation stated explicitly.
\item Settings manifest, open cases and a reporting checklist for ventricular FSI.
\end{highlights}
